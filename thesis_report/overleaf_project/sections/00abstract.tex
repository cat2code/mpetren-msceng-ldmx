\noindent

The \gls{ldmx} searches for sub-GeV dark matter particles
through a missing-momentum signature. Multiple electrons in one readout
window can produce overlapping electromagnetic-calorimeter showers and
obscure hit origins. This thesis studies supervised hit-level separation in
simulated two- and three-electron pile-up events.

Events are represented as unordered, variable-size sets of calorimeter hits
and trigger-pad-track observations. Transformer and GravNet are modern architectures that can 
assign each hit to its dominant origin electron. A multitask Transformer also
predicts electron count, per-hit contributor sets, and deposited-energy
fractions, inspired by particle-flow algorithms.

On matched test samples, the Transformer achieved permutation-aligned pooled
hit accuracies of \(84.20\,\%\) for two-electron events and \(74.84\,\%\)
for three-electron events, compared with \(83.79\,\%\) and \(74.01\,\%\),
respectively, for GravNet. The architectures therefore performed similarly,
with a small Transformer advantage in this campaign. The lower
three-electron accuracy is consistent with
the smaller closest-pair separation in that sample and the observed dependence
of model performance on shower overlap.
%while higher energy-weighted accuracies showed that misassigned hits tended to carry slightly less energy. 
Removing trigger-pad tokens at inference time produced a small but
statistically resolved change in fixed-multiplicity hit assignment and a
larger change in variable-multiplicity electron counting. These ablations do
not isolate useful trigger-pad information from the distribution shift caused
by removing a complete input modality. The final model achieved
restricted \(2e\)-versus-\(3e\) electron-count, contributor-set, and native
dominant-origin accuracies of
\(99.98\,\%\), \(72.41\,\%\), and \(77.04\,\%\), respectively.

Set-based neural architectures can thus recover substantial per-electron
shower structure from simulated \gls{ldmx} pile-up events, although mixed and
strongly overlapping deposits remain the principal challenge. The framework developed for this thesis is designed to be reusable and enables further studies with heterogeneous \gls{ldmx} detector inputs.
