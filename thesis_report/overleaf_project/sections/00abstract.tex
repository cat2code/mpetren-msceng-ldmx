\noindent

The \gls{ldmx} searches for sub-GeV dark matter through missing momentum.
Multiple electrons in one readout window can produce overlapping calorimeter
showers and obscure the origin of individual hits. This thesis evaluates
Transformer and GravNet for supervised hit-level reconstruction of simulated two- and three-electron
pile-up events represented as unordered variable-size detector-object sets.

On held-out test samples, the Transformer achieved permutation-invariant pooled
hit accuracies of \(84.20\,\%\) for two-electron events and \(74.84\,\%\)
for three-electron events, or $89.76\%$ and $83.18\%$ respectively for the energy weighted variants. GravNet performed similarly, with a small observed
Transformer advantage. The lower three-electron performance
is consistent with greater shower overlap.

The \gls{mlpf}-inspired \gls{trace} retained nearly the same
hit assignment performance as the baselines while also predicting electron slots,
contributor sets and energy fractions. It distinguished two from three
electrons with \(99.983\,\%\) accuracy, while exact contributor-set accuracy
was \(72.41\,\%\). Mixed deposits remained the main reconstruction challenge.

Inference-time trigger-pad ablations produced statistically resolved changes,
but could not distinguish useful detector information from the distribution
shift caused by removing a complete modality. The results establish set-based
learning as a feasible offline approach to per-electron reconstruction in
simulated \gls{ldmx} pile-up. The reusable \texttt{ml\_ldmx} framework
supports controlled detector-modality studies and future reconstruction tasks.
